📚 Learn — How GEX Trading Works
🏦 How This Tool Works (Plain English) ▾

One sentence: This tool calculates invisible price levels where Nifty is pulled toward or pushed away from, based on how market makers hedge their options books.

Who are market makers?

Market makers are professional firms that sell options to retail traders. When you buy a Nifty CE, someone (a market maker) sold it to you. To protect themselves, they hedge continuously — buying and selling the underlying as the market moves. This hedging creates invisible forces on the index.

The bouncer analogy: Nifty is in a room with a ceiling at GMax. Big players are like bouncers at a club — they keep selling near that ceiling level, just like a bouncer who won't let anyone past the velvet rope. You're on the bouncer's side when you sell at the ceiling. The ceiling doesn't physically stop anything, but the collective hedging pressure of all those market makers makes it very hard to break through.

The three key levels

🟢 Gamma Max (GMax)
The strike with the most total gamma × open interest. Nifty is magnetically attracted to GMax from 1–2% away (market makers delta-hedge toward it), but repelled AT the level (they sell every attempt to break through). Acts as a ceiling when above spot, a floor when below.
🔵 Delta Neutral (DN)
Where market makers' total delta exposure is exactly zero. Below DN they are net long delta (buy dips = supportive). Above DN they are net short delta (sell rallies = resistive). The gravitational center on quiet days. Good for range-bound strategies.
🟠 Gamma Neutral (GN)
Where total gamma flips from positive to negative. Above GN: market makers are long gamma — they dampen moves, big swings fade. Below GN: market makers are short gamma — they amplify moves, breakdowns accelerate. The regime boundary that tells you whether to fade or follow moves.
The safety net analogy: Think of Delta Neutral as a safety net stretched under a bouncing ball. The net bounces the ball back up when it falls toward it. Market makers collecting rent on that safety net — they keep buying when Nifty approaches DN from above, keeping it from falling further.

Hardy's +100% rule (for BUY plays)

Rule 1: Put maximum 1% of capital on any single play.
Rule 2: When the option doubles, sell HALF — you've recovered your full cost.

📊 Understanding the Backtest ▾

Methodology

  • Entry: Next morning's open price (Day D+1 open) — the price you'd actually pay
  • Exit (intraday): If bhavcopy high ≥ 2× entry → WIN. If low ≤ 0.5× entry → STOPPED OUT
  • Exit (EOD): If neither triggered intraday, use EOD settlement price
  • Slippage: 5% applied to both entry and exit to model bid-ask spread
  • VIX filter: No plays on days with VIX outside [14, 30]
  • Regime filter: SELL CE suppressed when spot > GMax (breakout mode)

Why the old method was wrong: An option could go from ₹200 to ₹400 at 11am then settle back at ₹210 at EOD. The old backtest called it NEUTRAL (+5%). The current methodology counts it as a WIN because it doubled intraday.

Result categories

WIN
Option doubled (+100%) intraday. Sold half at +100%, let rest ride. Clean outcome.
NEUTRAL
Gained but didn't double (+1% to +99%). Time decay + small move. Good outcome if you were selling.
SCRATCH
Small loss (0% to -33%). Stopped out but limited damage. Acceptable.
LOSS
Hit full stop loss (-50%). After VIX + regime fixes: 0 catastrophic losses in 6-month backtest.

⚠️ What This Tool Cannot Do ▾
  • ⏰ Uses yesterday's data — GEX levels are calculated from EOD bhavcopy. Intraday OI shifts can change levels meaningfully. Always verify direction at 9:15 AM before entering.
  • 👁 No intraday monitoring — You must watch for your exit conditions yourself (option doubling or hitting stop). This is not an algo — it generates the setup, you execute it.
  • 🏪 India F&O is 90% retail — GEX theory assumes market-maker dominance. On index options (Nifty) this holds well. On individual stocks, MMs are less dominant — results are weaker.
  • 📅 Expect 5–15 WIN days per 6 months — Most days will be NEUTRAL or SCRATCH. That's fine if you follow the +100% / -50% rule — wins are big, losses are small.
  • 🚫 Not financial advice — Always use defined-risk structures (spreads, not naked options). Never risk more than 1% of capital per play. Options can expire worthless.
